# R10 public-content brief — What makes a page relevant to a customer question rather than merely keyword-rich?

**State:** NOT_APPROVED_FOR_PUBLICATION. **Type:** evidence-led article/service-support brief. **Primary specialist:** R10. **Proposed author:** Joseph, subject to his review and approval; do not insert unverified credentials. This is not completed client work.

## Reader and purpose

A business owner or practitioner facing this specific problem needs a diagnosis and a way to evaluate professional help. The article should answer: **What makes a page relevant to a customer question rather than merely keyword-rich?**

**Distinct contribution:** A transparent relevance rubric and judged query examples, with evidence gaps and task completion instead of opaque SEO scores.

## Candidate search questions — hypotheses, not measured demand

- search intent page relevance audit
- information gain useful website content
- site search relevance evaluation

No keyword volumes, ranking difficulty, expected traffic, citation probability or sales forecast have been measured for these suggestions. R23 validates commercial relevance from real inquiries or authorized search data; R10 checks answer relevance. Merge with an existing page when its task is already answered well.

## Proposed outline

1. Separate owned retrieval from Google
2. Judge actual questions
3. Identify missing information
4. Evaluate without claiming hidden weights

Open with a direct bounded answer. Explain the observable distinction that matters, demonstrate the method with an authorized artifact or an explicitly hypothetical example, and close with practical limitations. Do not clone this outline across 26 pages by changing only role names.

## Evidence needed before making an expertise or results claim

- Query set with observed versus hypothesis labels
- Relevance rubric and judged examples

An unmeasured benefit must remain a hypothesis.
